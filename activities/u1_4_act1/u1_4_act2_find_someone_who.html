1.

Use the graph to analyze continuity at $x=-2$.

  1. State $\lim_{x\to-2}f(x)$.
  2. State $f(-2)$.
  3. Decide whether $f$ is continuous at $x=-2$ and identify the failed condition.
2.

Let $f(x)=\begin{cases}2x+1,&x\lt3\k,&x=3\x+4,&x\gt3\end{cases}$.

  1. Find the left-hand and right-hand limits at $x=3$.
  2. Determine whether any value of $k$ can make $f$ continuous at $3$.
  3. Justify the conclusion.
3.

Classify each discontinuity.

  1. The graph approaches the same finite value from both sides, but the point is missing.
  2. The left- and right-hand limits are finite but unequal.
  3. The function grows without bound as $x$ approaches the point.
4.

A student checks continuity at $x=1$ and reports only that $f(1)=5$.

  1. List the other information still needed.
  2. Write the equality that must hold for continuity.
  3. Explain why knowing the function value alone is insufficient.
5.

Suppose a function is continuous on the open interval $(0,6)$ and is right-continuous at $0$ and left-continuous at $6$.

  1. What continuity conclusion can be made on $[0,6]$?
  2. Explain why the endpoint checks are one-sided.
