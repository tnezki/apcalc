Station 1 Answers

Question 1

Vertical $x=2$; horizontal $y=-1$; for example $\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=-1$.
Rationale: Read the function behavior near the indicated x-value or at the far end of the graph; unbounded behavior identifies an infinite limit and the corresponding asymptote.

Question 2

$x=-3$
Rationale: Identify where the function becomes unbounded as x approaches a finite value; after simplification, a noncanceling denominator zero indicates the vertical asymptote.

Question 3

The function is continuous on [a,b], and N lies between f(a) and f(b).
Rationale: Verify continuity on the closed interval and check that the target output lies between the endpoint outputs; those are the hypotheses needed to invoke the Intermediate Value Theorem.

Question 4

No. IVT guarantees existence, not uniqueness.
Rationale: Verify continuity on the closed interval and check that the target output lies between the endpoint outputs; those are the hypotheses needed to invoke the Intermediate Value Theorem.

Question 5

Yes. A horizontal asymptote describes end behavior, not a barrier the graph can never cross.
Rationale: Compare the dominant terms as x grows without bound. Their ratio determines the finite end-behavior limit and any horizontal asymptote.
