Whiteboard Indy
Asymptotes and IVT
StructureIndividual -> Independent practice
SetupEach student gets a whiteboard and one marker.
Directions
  1. Notes are welcome.
  2. Try something.
  3. Write large and clearly — your work should be readable from across the room.
  4. Use your resources: ask your partner first, then the teacher. Do not copy.
  5. Mistakes are part of learning — fix them and discuss what went wrong.
Goal: Individual accountability + low-stakes problem entry

Set 2 — Practice Set 3

Looks Like Success
  • Every student has their own board and begins independently.
  • Work is large enough to read when boards are raised.
  • Students revise mistakes after checking or discussion.
Doesn't Look Like
  • Copying a neighbor before trying the problem.
  • Tiny work that cannot be read from across the room.
  • Erasing an error without discussing or correcting it.

  5. Rotate partners when directed.
Goal: Verbal reasoning + procedural / conceptual accuracy

Set 2 — Practice Set 3

Unit 1 · Section 5 · Activity 1
Speed Dating Math
Asymptotes and IVT
StructurePaired practice -> Timed rotation -> Strategy sharing
SetupPartners. One whiteboard or blank paper for each person.
Directions
  1. Work with a partner on the projected problem — both students write on their own board or paper.
  2. Compare strategies, evidence, representations, or methods before rotating.
  3. After 3–4 minutes, one row shifts one seat to create a new pairing.
  4. Bring your previous work and share your reasoning with your new partner.
  5. Repeat across multiple problems and partner pairings.
Goal: Multiple representations + collaborative problem solving
Question Sets

Set 1 — Practice Set 2
Set 2 — Practice Set 3

Looks Like Success
  • Both partners record their own work before comparing.
  • Pairs compare methods before the rotation signal.
  • Students bring prior work and explain it to the new partner.
Doesn't Look Like
  • Only one person in the pair writes.
  • Students rotate before comparing strategies.
  • Previous work is abandoned instead of used for discussion.
Unit 1 · Section 5 · Activity 1
Showdown
Asymptotes and IVT
StructureIndy -> Group comparison -> Consensus
SetupGroups of 4. Each person has a whiteboard. Rotate the Captain each round.
Directions
  1. Each student solves the problem individually.
  2. When prompted, students Showdown — reveal answers at the same time.
  3. Discuss differences and justify reasoning.
  4. The group agrees on a correct final answer or explanation.
  5. Repeat.
Goal: Accountability + justification
Question Sets

Set 1 — Practice Set 2
Set 2 — Practice Set 3

Looks Like Success
  • Every student completes a board before the reveal signal.
  • Boards are revealed at the same time.
  • The group resolves differences with mathematical justification.
Doesn't Look Like
  • Students show answers early to copy.
  • Only the Captain solves the problem.
  • The group votes without discussing conflicting reasoning.

Think, Trade, Agree
Asymptotes and IVT
StructureIndy -> Group comparison -> Consensus
SetupGroups of 4. Each person has a whiteboard.
Directions
  1. Teacher presents a problem.
  2. Each student works independently first.
  3. When everyone is ready, exchange boards with another student.
  4. Discuss differences and justify reasoning.
  5. Come to an agreement or ask the teacher for help.
  6. Repeat the exchange with someone different.
Goal: Accountability + justification
Question Sets

Set 1 — Practice Set 2
Set 2 — Practice Set 3

Looks Like Success
  • Everyone works independently before boards are traded.
  • Students inspect a classmate's reasoning, not only the final answer.
  • The group reaches an agreement or identifies a specific question for the teacher.
Doesn't Look Like
  • Boards are traded before independent work is attempted.
  • Students change answers without explaining why.
  • The same two students trade every round.
Unit 1 · Section 5 · Activity 1
Round Table
Asymptotes and IVT
StructureOne step / contribution per student
SetupGroups of 4. One whiteboard and one marker per group.
Directions
  1. Groups of 4 use one whiteboard and marker.
  2. Each student completes one meaningful step, representation, observation, or reasoning move, then passes the board.
  3. Rotate until the problem is complete.
  4. Compare when the solution is shown.
  5. Make sure every group member can explain the final reasoning.
Goal: Sequential reasoning + shared responsibility
Question Sets

Set 1 — Practice Set 2
Set 2 — Practice Set 3

Looks Like Success
  • Each student adds one meaningful step before passing the board.
  • The board moves through all group members.
  • Every group member can explain the finished reasoning.
Doesn't Look Like
  • One student completes several steps before passing.
  • Students add unrelated marks just to take a turn.
  • Only the final writer understands the solution.

Stations
Asymptotes and IVT
StructureTimed rotations
Setup8 stations around the room. Pick up a record sheet or work paper before you begin.
Directions
  1. Choose a starting station with no more than 4 students.
  2. Work on the questions at that station.
  3. Rotate when the teacher signals.
  4. Do not all move to the same next station.
  5. Keep your own record of work and corrections.
Goal: Repetition + varied problem exposure

  • Everyone follows the same group to the same next station.
Unit 1 · Section 5 · Activity 1
Find Someone Who
Asymptotes and IVT
StructureMovement + peer interaction
SetupKeep your worksheet and pencil with you as you move around the room.
Directions
  1. Work alone first: skim every problem, then begin any problem you can solve.
  2. Find a partner who is ready to share. Compare completed answers and discuss any that do not match.
  3. Choose one problem that either of you left incomplete. Work through it together. Both partners record the reasoning on their own sheet.
  4. Thank your partner and repeat with a different person.
  5. Return to your seat with every problem completed and understood — not merely filled in.
Goal: Exposure to multiple explanations

Set 2 — Find Someone Who

Looks Like Success
  • Students attempt problems independently before seeking a partner.
  • Partners compare completed work and jointly finish one incomplete item.
  • Each student records the reasoning on their own worksheet.
Doesn't Look Like
  • Students collect answers without discussing reasoning.
  • One partner writes on the other person's sheet.
  • Students repeatedly work with the same partner.

Tarsia Puzzle
Asymptotes and IVT
StructureMatching puzzle (self-checking)
SetupGroups of 4. Use a teacher-prepared Tarsia puzzle generated from the section's Tarsia TXT.
Directions
  1. Match questions, expressions, representations, or answers on adjoining puzzle edges.
  2. Continue until the pieces form the complete intended shape.
  3. If the puzzle does not fit, locate and discuss the mismatch rather than forcing pieces together.
Goal: Error detection + pattern recognition
Teacher Note

Use the section's Tarsia TXT from the dashboard with the separate master Tarsia generator.

Looks Like Success
  • Groups justify each edge match before placing a piece.
  • Mismatched pieces trigger discussion and rechecking.
  • The final shape is built from mathematically consistent matches.
Doesn't Look Like
  • Pieces are forced together because their shapes fit.
  • One student assembles while others watch.
  • A mismatch is ignored instead of traced to a question or answer.
Unit 1 · Section 5 · Activity 1
Mathematical Hot Seat
Asymptotes and IVT
StructureVerbal description only -> Blind solving -> Role rotation
SetupGroups. One whiteboard and one marker per member.
Directions
  1. One student per team sits facing away from the screen — the Hot Seat.
  2. The teacher projects a problem that only teammates can see.
  3. Teammates describe the problem using words only — no gestures and no copying the displayed work.
  4. The Hot Seat student solves or explains based solely on the verbal description.
  5. After solving, the student turns around and compares to the original problem.
  6. Rotate the Hot Seat for each round.
Goal: Precise academic vocabulary + communication
Question Sets

Set 1 — Practice Set 2
Set 2 — Practice Set 3

Looks Like Success
  • The Hot Seat student faces away from the projected problem.
  • Teammates use precise words without gestures or copying displayed notation.
  • The Hot Seat role rotates after each round.
Doesn't Look Like
  • The Hot Seat student turns to look at the screen early.
  • Teammates point, gesture, or copy the displayed work.
  • The same student stays in the Hot Seat.

Desmos / Digital Math Activity
Asymptotes and IVT
StructureIndividual or Partners -> Teacher-paced digital investigation
SetupIndividual or partner work on a computer/iPad.
Directions
  1. Open the teacher-selected activity using the posted link or code.
  2. Read each prompt carefully; screens may ask you to type, sketch, move sliders, or plot points.
  3. Work at the pace set by the teacher.
  4. Stop and look up when the teacher pauses the class for discussion.
  5. Show your thinking honestly in the activity responses.
Goal: Conceptual exploration + interactive sense-making
Teacher Note

This is an optional teacher-selected digital activity. Do not invent a course-specific URL.

Looks Like Success
  • Students open the teacher-selected activity and follow its prompts.
  • Students stop with the class when the teacher pauses for discussion.
  • Responses show the student's own mathematical thinking.
Doesn't Look Like
  • Students browse unrelated screens or activities.
  • Students rush ahead during a teacher pause.
  • Students submit copied responses without showing their reasoning.
