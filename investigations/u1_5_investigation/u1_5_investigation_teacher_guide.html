Why This Investigation Fits Section 1.5

The current section writes, solves, checks, and interprets one-variable equations in context. Comparing the two park cost models creates a natural exact-equality question without introducing a new system-solving method: students set two expressions equal and solve one equation in $r$.

Equation: $8+3r=2+5r$.

Solution: $6=2r$, so $r=3$ rides. Both parks cost $17$ at 3 rides.

Before 3 rides Park B is cheaper; after 3 rides Park A is cheaper.

Watch for: saying $r=3$ means 3 dollars, adding the two cost expressions instead of setting them equal, or solving without interpreting.

Strong Synthesis

Tables efficiently check specific inputs, graphs show overall trends and approximate intersections, equations can determine exact equality, and the context supplies units and meaning.
