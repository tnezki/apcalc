AP Calculus AB — Unit 2: Derivative Basics

Essential Standard / Enduring Understanding

The derivative is a limit that measures instantaneous change and tangent slope, and derivative rules provide efficient ways to compute that change while preserving meaning, notation, and differentiability conditions.

Section 2.1: Average to Instant Rate

AP Topics: 2.1

Learning Target: Connect average rate of change and secant slopes to instantaneous rate of change and tangent slope estimates.

I Can Statements

  • I can connect average rate of change and secant slopes to instantaneous rate of change and tangent slope estimates.
  • I can estimate tangent slope from shrinking intervals.
  • I can interpret average and instantaneous rates in a shared context.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • average rate of change
  • instantaneous rate of change
  • secant line
  • tangent line
  • difference quotient

Key Representations

  • secant and tangent line graphs
  • difference quotient expressions
  • tables of shrinking intervals

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Using a single secant slope as the exact instantaneous rate.
  • Confusing change in output with average rate of change.
  • Losing the interval width in a difference quotient.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Estimate tangent slope from shrinking intervals.
  • Interpret average and instantaneous rates in a shared context.

Section 2.3: Differentiability

AP Topics: 2.4

Learning Target: Determine where a function is differentiable and connect differentiability to continuity, corners, cusps, vertical tangents, and discontinuities.

I Can Statements

  • I can determine where a function is differentiable and connect differentiability to continuity, corners, cusps, vertical tangents, and discontinuities.
  • I can classify points of nondifferentiability from graphs.
  • I can justify why a function can be continuous but not differentiable at a point.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • differentiability
  • continuity
  • corner
  • cusp
  • vertical tangent

Key Representations

  • graphs with nondifferentiable points
  • local tangent behavior
  • continuity/differentiability implication statements

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Assuming continuity guarantees differentiability.
  • Treating a vertical tangent as an ordinary finite derivative.
  • Missing nondifferentiability at corners or cusps on an otherwise continuous graph.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Classify points of nondifferentiability from graphs.
  • Justify why a function can be continuous but not differentiable at a point.

Recurring Practice Opportunities

  • Representation connection: Use the section’s graphical/numerical/symbolic representations to support the same mathematical conclusion and explain any apparent mismatch.
  • Justification / conditions: Identify the mathematical condition or evidence that supports the conclusion rather than naming a rule or theorem without support.
  • Modeling: Translate between the stated situation and a mathematical model/diagram, preserving the meaning of variables, dimensions, and relationships.
