Section Introduction

Derivatives of Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

The exponential function $e^x$ is special because it is its own derivative. Natural logarithm is its inverse, giving the equally important derivative $1/x$.

Logarithms also provide a technique for simplifying difficult differentiation problems before taking the derivative.

RulesExponential and Logarithmic Derivatives
$(e^u)'=e^u u'$$(a^u)'=a^u\ln(a)u'$$(\ln u)'=\dfrac{u'}{u}$$(\log_a u)'=\dfrac{u'}{u\ln a}$
TechniqueLogarithmic Differentiation
  1. Take $\ln$ of both sides.
  2. Use log properties to expand products, quotients, and powers.
  3. Differentiate implicitly.
  4. Solve for $y'$ and substitute the original $y$ if needed.
ConnectionGrowth and Spread

When a model contains exponential growth or decay, the derivative gives the instantaneous rate at which the quantity is changing.
