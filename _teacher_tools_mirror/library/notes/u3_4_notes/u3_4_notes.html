Section Introduction

Velocity and Other Rates of Change

In calculus, an unspecified “rate of change” usually means an instantaneous rate of change. That is a derivative.

For motion on a line, the sign of velocity tells direction while speed ignores direction. Acceleration describes how velocity itself changes.

What's To Come

PREVIEW. Every example below is chosen to move from meaning to efficient calculation and then back to interpretation.

MotionPosition, Velocity, Acceleration

If position is $s(t)$, then

$$v(t)=s'(t),\qquad a(t)=v'(t)=s''(t).$$

Speed is $|v(t)|$. Positive velocity means motion in the positive direction; negative velocity means motion in the negative direction.

InterpretationSpeeding Up vs. Slowing Down

An object speeds up when velocity and acceleration have the same sign. It slows down when they have opposite signs.

ApplicationMarginal Change

If $C(x)$ is a cost function, then $C'(x)$ is the marginal cost. Near production level $x$, it approximates the added cost of producing one more unit.

Example 1 · Position to motion

A particle has position $s(t)=-16t^2+16t+320$. Find displacement from $t=1$ to $t=2$, velocity, speed at impact, and acceleration.

Example 2 · Read a velocity graph

A velocity graph crosses the $t$-axis at $t=2$ and $t=6$. Explain how to determine when the particle moves left, moves right, stops, and changes direction.

Example 3 · Speeding up

If $v(4)<0$ and $a(4)<0$, is the particle speeding up or slowing down? Explain using signs.

Example 4 · Geometric rate

The length of a rectangle is $2t+1$ and its height is $\sqrt t$. Find $dA/dt$ and give units.

Example 5 · Marginal cost

Let $C(x)=0.002x^3+0.1x^2+42x+300$. Interpret $C'(40)$ in context.
