Section Introduction

Implicit Differentiation

An implicit equation such as $x^2+y^2=4$ describes a curve without solving for $y$. We can still find the slope directly.

The key is to remember that $y$ depends on $x$, so differentiating a power of $y$ requires the Chain Rule.

What's To Come

PREVIEW. Every example below is chosen to move from meaning to efficient calculation and then back to interpretation.

ProcedureImplicit Differentiation
  1. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$.
  2. Whenever you differentiate a $y$-expression, multiply by $dy/dx$.
  3. Collect all $dy/dx$ terms on one side.
  4. Factor and solve for $dy/dx$.
Example RuleDifferentiating $y$-Terms

Because $y=y(x)$,

$$\frac{d}{dx}(y^3)=3y^2\frac{dy}{dx},$$

and for a product,

$$\frac{d}{dx}(xy)=x\frac{dy}{dx}+y.$$
InterpretationHorizontal and Vertical Tangents

After solving for $dy/dx=N/D$, horizontal tangents occur where $N=0$ and $D\ne0$; vertical tangents occur where $D=0$ and $N\ne0$.

Example 1 · Circle

For $x^2+y^2=4$, find $dy/dx$ implicitly and determine the slope at $(\sqrt3,1)$.

Example 2 · Product term

Find $dy/dx$ for $x^3+y^3=6xy$.

Example 3 · Tangent and normal

For $x^3+y^3=6xy$, find the tangent and normal lines at $(4/3,8/3)$.

Example 4 · Trig implicit equation

Find $dy/dx$ at $(0,0)$ if $\tan(x+y)=x$.

Example 5 · Horizontal/vertical tangent

For $x^2+xy+y^2=6$, determine where horizontal or vertical tangents can occur.
