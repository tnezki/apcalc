AP Calculus AB — Unit 8: Integration Applications

Essential Standard / Enduring Understanding

Definite integrals solve applied accumulation problems by averaging quantities, tracking motion, measuring area between curves, and modeling volumes through cross sections or rotation with careful setup and interpretation.

Section Outline

Section 8.1: Average Value and Net Change

Learning Target: Use definite integrals to determine average value and net change, and distinguish average value from average rate of change in contextual situations.

I Can Statements

  • I can use definite integrals to determine average value and net change, and distinguish average value from average rate of change in contextual situations.
  • I can compute and interpret average value.
  • I can use a rate function to determine net change.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • average value
  • average rate of change
  • net change
  • definite integral

Key Representations

  • function/rate graphs
  • tables
  • definite integral formulas
  • contextual interpretations

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Using the average rate of change formula for average value.
  • Confusing net change with total accumulated amount.
  • Ignoring interval length in average value.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Compute and interpret average value.
  • Use a rate function to determine net change.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Use definite integrals to determine average value and net change, and distinguish average value from average rate of change in contextual situations.

Last Focus: Model and interpret exponential growth and decay from differential equations in contextual situations.

Mastery Priorities: Definite-integral meaning; accumulation and antiderivatives; graph/table interpretation; algebraic and geometric setup; units.

Section 8.2: Motion with Integrals

Learning Target: Use integrals to determine displacement, total distance, and position from velocity, while distinguishing velocity from speed and accounting for direction changes.

I Can Statements

  • I can use integrals to determine displacement, total distance, and position from velocity, while distinguishing velocity from speed and accounting for direction changes.
  • I can compute displacement and total distance from a velocity model.
  • I can recover position from velocity and an initial condition.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • displacement
  • total distance
  • velocity
  • speed
  • position

Key Representations

  • velocity graphs
  • integrals of velocity
  • motion tables
  • sign-change intervals

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Using signed displacement as total distance.
  • Failing to split an integral where velocity changes sign for total distance.
  • Forgetting an initial position when finding position from accumulated velocity.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Compute displacement and total distance from a velocity model.
  • Recover position from velocity and an initial condition.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Use integrals to determine displacement, total distance, and position from velocity, while distinguishing velocity from speed and accounting for direction changes.

Last Focus: Use definite integrals to determine average value and net change, and distinguish average value from average rate of change in contextual situations.

Section 8.3: Area Between Curves

Learning Target: Set up and evaluate area between curves with respect to $x$ or $y$, including multiple intersections and orientation decisions.

I Can Statements

  • I can set up and evaluate area between curves with respect to $x$ or $y$, including multiple intersections and orientation decisions.
  • I can set up area with respect to $x$ or $y$.
  • I can compare alternative slice orientations for the same region.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • area between curves
  • upper/lower function
  • right/left function
  • intersection

Key Representations

  • graphs of bounding curves
  • vertical slices
  • horizontal slices
  • definite integrals

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Subtracting in the wrong order and getting negative geometric area.
  • Using $dx$ when a $dy$ setup is structurally more appropriate without rewriting functions.
  • Missing a necessary split at an intersection where the bounding order changes.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Set up area with respect to $x$ or $y$.
  • Compare alternative slice orientations for the same region.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Set up and evaluate area between curves with respect to $x$ or $y$, including multiple intersections and orientation decisions.

Last Focus: Use integrals to determine displacement, total distance, and position from velocity, while distinguishing velocity from speed and accounting for direction changes.

Mastery Priorities: Definite-integral meaning; accumulation and antiderivatives; graph/table interpretation; algebraic and geometric setup; units.

Section 8.4: Volumes with Cross Sections

Learning Target: Model volumes with known cross-sectional shapes by converting a base region into an area function and integrating over the appropriate interval.

I Can Statements

  • I can model volumes with known cross-sectional shapes by converting a base region into an area function and integrating over the appropriate interval.
  • I can build a cross-sectional area function from a base region.
  • I can set up volumes for square, rectangular, triangular, or semicircular cross sections.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • cross section
  • base region
  • area function
  • volume

Key Representations

  • base-region graphs
  • cross-sectional diagrams
  • area formulas
  • volume integrals

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Integrating a length instead of cross-sectional area.
  • Using the wrong dimension as the side/base/diameter of a cross section.
  • Forgetting that the area formula may square a distance between curves.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Build a cross-sectional area function from a base region.
  • Set up volumes for square, rectangular, triangular, or semicircular cross sections.

Spiral Planning

Section 8.5: Volumes by Rotation

Learning Target: Set up disc and washer volume integrals about coordinate or shifted axes, making correct radius and orientation decisions.

I Can Statements

  • I can set up disc and washer volume integrals about coordinate or shifted axes, making correct radius and orientation decisions.
  • I can set up disc or washer integrals about varied axes.
  • I can compare radius expressions for coordinate versus shifted axes.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • disc method
  • washer method
  • outer radius
  • inner radius
  • axis of rotation

Key Representations

  • regions with rotation axes
  • disc/washer slices
  • radius expressions
  • volume integrals

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Using a function value as a radius without measuring distance to the axis of rotation.
  • Reversing inner and outer radii.
  • Choosing a slice orientation that does not generate discs or washers for the stated axis.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Set up disc or washer integrals about varied axes.
  • Compare radius expressions for coordinate versus shifted axes.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Set up disc and washer volume integrals about coordinate or shifted axes, making correct radius and orientation decisions.

Last Focus: Model volumes with known cross-sectional shapes by converting a base region into an area function and integrating over the appropriate interval.

Mastery Priorities: Definite-integral meaning; accumulation and antiderivatives; graph/table interpretation; algebraic and geometric setup; units.

Unit Themes / Throughlines

Unit Themes / Throughlines
  • Distinguish net change, total distance, average value, and average rate of change.
  • Use diagrams and interval analysis to decide integral setup.
  • Choose $dx$ or $dy$ based on geometric structure and bounding relationships.
  • Convert geometric dimensions into cross-sectional area functions before integrating.
  • Measure radii from the axis of rotation and justify disc/washer setup choices.

Summative Assessment Plan

Unit 8 Summative Assessment Eligible after Section 8.5, with timing adjusted to the framework pacing and instructional needs.

Recommended format: A course-appropriate AP-style mixed assessment using selected-response and constructed-response/FRQ-style evidence, with calculator or no-calculator conditions when the relevant framework section calls for them. The plan does not prescribe a fixed paper-only length.

Major evidence priorities: average value and net change; motion with integrals; area between curves; cross-sectional volume; and disc/washer volume.

Representation / reasoning / interpretation expectations: Students should work across the representations emphasized in the unit, use precise notation, justify conclusions when conditions matter, and include units or contextual interpretation when the framework makes them relevant.

Conventional summative questions should use DOK levels that naturally fit the assessed content. DOK 4 evidence is normally collected through performance-oriented modeling, investigation, design, synthesis, or transfer tasks rather than forced into ordinary test questions.

Spiral / retention statement: Include deliberate retrieval of earlier prerequisite ideas that remain active in the unit, especially notation, representation interpretation, and previously established calculus relationships; do not treat spiral work as random review.

Holistic Rubric

A — Highly Proficient

The student independently constructs and interprets definite-integral models for average value, motion, area, and volume across graphical and geometric representations. Work is accurate, well-organized, and supported by clear reasoning. The student connects relevant representations, concepts, evidence, or models and communicates conclusions with precision and independence.

B — Proficient

The student generally demonstrates proficiency with average value and net change; motion with integrals; area between curves; cross-sectional volume; and disc/washer volume. Most work is accurate, and the student can complete standard and moderately complex tasks independently. Reasoning and communication are generally clear, though they may lack some precision, flexibility, or depth.

C — Developing Proficiency

The student demonstrates developing proficiency with average value and net change; motion with integrals; area between curves; cross-sectional volume; and disc/washer volume. The student can complete many routine tasks but may need support with reasoning, representations, notation, evidence, modeling, or multi-step problem solving. Work may contain errors or incomplete explanations, but there is sufficient evidence of developing understanding of the central ideas.

Not Yet — Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet demonstrate consistent proficiency with average value and net change; motion with integrals; area between curves; cross-sectional volume; and disc/washer volume. Work shows major misconceptions, significant procedural or conceptual errors, weak reasoning, or difficulty connecting the representations, evidence, or models required by the unit. Additional instruction, practice, feedback, or reassessment may be needed before proficiency is demonstrated.
