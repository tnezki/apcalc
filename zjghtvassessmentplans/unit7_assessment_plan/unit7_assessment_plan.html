AP Calculus AB — Unit 7: Differential Equations

Essential Standard / Enduring Understanding

Differential equations model how quantities change; slope fields visualize local behavior, separation of variables produces solution families, and initial conditions or exponential structures determine contextual particular solutions.

Unit Source Alignment

Framework Alignment
  • Framework Topics / Standards: 7.1: AP Topics 7.1, 7.2; 7.2: AP Topics 7.3, 7.4; 7.3: AP Topics 7.6; 7.4: AP Topics 7.7; 7.5: AP Topics 7.8.
  • Course-Specific Emphasis: College Board AP Calculus AB topic sequence supported by teacher-created warm-ups, guided notes, investigations, cooperative learning, AP Classroom practice, optional FRQ-style writing, and continual attention to justification, notation, units, and interpretation.
  • Pacing Guidance: The framework suggests approximately 7-8 instructional days for Unit 7, but sections may be compressed, extended, reordered, or supported with optional resources as needed; optional third-day work is not a required calendar.

Section Outline

Section 7.1: Modeling Differential Equations

Learning Target: Translate verbal rate relationships into differential equations and verify whether proposed functions satisfy a differential equation.

I Can Statements

  • I can translate verbal rate relationships into differential equations and verify whether proposed functions satisfy a differential equation.
  • I can model a verbal growth or change statement with a differential equation.
  • I can verify a proposed solution by substitution.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • differential equation
  • solution
  • rate relationship
  • verification

Key Representations

  • verbal statements
  • differential equations
  • candidate solution functions

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Writing an algebraic equation instead of a rate equation.
  • Checking a candidate function value without checking its derivative relationship.
  • Ignoring variable dependence in a contextual differential equation.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Model a verbal growth or change statement with a differential equation.
  • Verify a proposed solution by substitution.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Translate verbal rate relationships into differential equations and verify whether proposed functions satisfy a differential equation.

Last Focus: Use $u$-substitution as chain-rule reversal and select among substitution, algebraic rewriting, long division, or completing the square when appropriate.

Mastery Priorities: Derivative and antiderivative relationships; algebraic manipulation; function notation; graph interpretation and units.

  • I can sketch a solution curve through an initial point.

  • solution curve
  • differential equation

Key Representations

  • slope fields
  • solution curves
  • differential equations

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Treating slope-field segments as a single connected solution curve.
  • Using the point coordinates rather than the differential equation to determine local slope.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

Summative Assessment Plan

Unit 7 Summative Assessment Eligible after Section 7.5, with timing adjusted to the framework pacing and instructional needs.

Recommended format: A course-appropriate AP-style mixed assessment using selected-response and constructed-response/FRQ-style evidence, with calculator or no-calculator conditions when the relevant framework section calls for them. The plan does not prescribe a fixed paper-only length.

Major evidence priorities: differential-equation modeling, slope fields, separation of variables, particular solutions, and exponential growth/decay models.

Representation / reasoning / interpretation expectations: Students should work across the representations emphasized in the unit, use precise notation, justify conclusions when conditions matter, and include units or contextual interpretation when the framework makes them relevant.

Conventional summative questions should use DOK levels that naturally fit the assessed content. DOK 4 evidence is normally collected through performance-oriented modeling, investigation, design, synthesis, or transfer tasks rather than forced into ordinary test questions.

Spiral / retention statement: Include deliberate retrieval of earlier prerequisite ideas that remain active in the unit, especially notation, representation interpretation, and previously established calculus relationships; do not treat spiral work as random review.

Holistic Rubric

A — Highly Proficient

The student independently models rate relationships with differential equations and connects slope fields, separated solutions, initial conditions, and exponential behavior. Work is accurate, well-organized, and supported by clear reasoning. The student connects relevant representations, concepts, evidence, or models and communicates conclusions with precision and independence.

B — Proficient

The student generally demonstrates proficiency with differential-equation modeling, slope fields, separation of variables, particular solutions, and exponential growth/decay models. Most work is accurate, and the student can complete standard and moderately complex tasks independently. Reasoning and communication are generally clear, though they may lack some precision, flexibility, or depth.

C — Developing Proficiency

The student demonstrates developing proficiency with differential-equation modeling, slope fields, separation of variables, particular solutions, and exponential growth/decay models. The student can complete many routine tasks but may need support with reasoning, representations, notation, evidence, modeling, or multi-step problem solving. Work may contain errors or incomplete explanations, but there is sufficient evidence of developing understanding of the central ideas.

Not Yet — Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet demonstrate consistent proficiency with differential-equation modeling, slope fields, separation of variables, particular solutions, and exponential growth/decay models. Work shows major misconceptions, significant procedural or conceptual errors, weak reasoning, or difficulty connecting the representations, evidence, or models required by the unit. Additional instruction, practice, feedback, or reassessment may be needed before proficiency is demonstrated.
