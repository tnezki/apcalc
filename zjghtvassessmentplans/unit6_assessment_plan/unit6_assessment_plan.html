AP Calculus AB — Unit 6: Integration and Accumulation

Essential Standard / Enduring Understanding

Definite integrals measure accumulation and signed change, Riemann sums approximate that accumulation, and the Fundamental Theorem connects accumulation, derivatives, and antiderivatives through strategic integration methods.

Unit Source Alignment

Framework Alignment
  • Framework Topics / Standards: 6.1: AP Topics 6.1, 6.6; 6.2: AP Topics 6.2, 6.3; 6.3: AP Topics 6.4, 6.5; 6.4: AP Topics 6.7, 6.8; 6.5: AP Topics 6.9, 6.10, 6.14.

  • Pacing Guidance: The framework suggests approximately 14-15 instructional days for Unit 6, but sections may be compressed, extended, reordered, or supported with optional resources as needed; optional third-day work is not a required calendar.

Section Outline

Section 6.1: Accumulation and Area

Learning Target: Interpret accumulation of change from rate graphs using signed area, units, and properties of definite integrals.

I Can Statements

  • I can interpret accumulation of change from rate graphs using signed area, units, and properties of definite integrals.
  • I can estimate accumulation from geometric regions.
  • I can interpret signed area as net change in a context.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • accumulation
  • signed area
  • definite integral
  • net change

Key Representations

  • rate graphs
  • geometric regions
  • definite integral notation
  • units

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Treating all geometric area as positive when the integral is signed.
  • Confusing accumulated change with final value when an initial value is needed.
  • Dropping units when integrating a rate.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Estimate accumulation from geometric regions.
  • Interpret signed area as net change in a context.

Spiral Planning

Last Focus: Model optimization situations with quantities, constraints, and objective functions, then use calculus-based reasoning to identify and justify optimal values.

Mastery Priorities: Derivative meaning and rules; graph/table interpretation; algebraic manipulation; notation and units.

Key Representations

  • graphs
  • rectangles/trapezoids

  • I can analyze an accumulation function from an integrand graph.

  • Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
  • accumulation function
  • definite integral

Key Representations

  • accumulation-function graphs
  • integral-defined functions
  • rate graphs
  • derivative relationships

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Ignoring a variable upper limit when differentiating an accumulation function.
  • Confusing the accumulation function value with the integrand value.

  • I can select an integration strategy from integrand structure.
  • I can compare substitution and algebraic-preparation approaches.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • $u$-substitution
  • change of variable
  • chain rule reversal
  • integration strategy

Key Representations

  • nested integrands
  • substitution notation
  • rewritten rational or quadratic forms

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Choosing $u$ without accounting for $du$.
  • Changing variables but leaving mixed $x$ and $u$ terms.
  • Using substitution when algebraic rewriting is the more direct structure.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Select an integration strategy from integrand structure.
  • Compare substitution and algebraic-preparation approaches.

Unit Themes / Throughlines

Unit Themes / Throughlines
  • Interpret integrals as accumulation with units and sign.
  • Connect numerical approximations, geometry, graphs, and symbolic integrals.
  • Use the Fundamental Theorem to link derivative and integral processes.
  • Maintain distinction between definite values and antiderivative families.
  • Select integration strategies by recognizing structure and chain-rule reversal.

Summative Assessment Plan

Unit 6 Summative Assessment Eligible after Section 6.5, with timing adjusted to the framework pacing and instructional needs.

Recommended format: A course-appropriate AP-style mixed assessment using selected-response and constructed-response/FRQ-style evidence, with calculator or no-calculator conditions when the relevant framework section calls for them. The plan does not prescribe a fixed paper-only length.

Major evidence priorities: accumulation, Riemann sums, FTC relationships, antiderivatives, and integration strategy including $u$-substitution.

Representation / reasoning / interpretation expectations: Students should work across the representations emphasized in the unit, use precise notation, justify conclusions when conditions matter, and include units or contextual interpretation when the framework makes them relevant.

Conventional summative questions should use DOK levels that naturally fit the assessed content. DOK 4 evidence is normally collected through performance-oriented modeling, investigation, design, synthesis, or transfer tasks rather than forced into ordinary test questions.

Spiral / retention statement: Include deliberate retrieval of earlier prerequisite ideas that remain active in the unit, especially notation, representation interpretation, and previously established calculus relationships; do not treat spiral work as random review.

Holistic Rubric

A — Highly Proficient

The student independently interprets and computes accumulation using numerical, graphical, and symbolic integral methods while connecting derivatives and antiderivatives through the FTC. Work is accurate, well-organized, and supported by clear reasoning. The student connects relevant representations, concepts, evidence, or models and communicates conclusions with precision and independence.

B — Proficient

The student generally demonstrates proficiency with accumulation, Riemann sums, FTC relationships, antiderivatives, and integration strategy including $u$-substitution. Most work is accurate, and the student can complete standard and moderately complex tasks independently. Reasoning and communication are generally clear, though they may lack some precision, flexibility, or depth.

C — Developing Proficiency

The student demonstrates developing proficiency with accumulation, Riemann sums, FTC relationships, antiderivatives, and integration strategy including $u$-substitution. The student can complete many routine tasks but may need support with reasoning, representations, notation, evidence, modeling, or multi-step problem solving. Work may contain errors or incomplete explanations, but there is sufficient evidence of developing understanding of the central ideas.

Not Yet — Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet demonstrate consistent proficiency with accumulation, Riemann sums, FTC relationships, antiderivatives, and integration strategy including $u$-substitution. Work shows major misconceptions, significant procedural or conceptual errors, weak reasoning, or difficulty connecting the representations, evidence, or models required by the unit. Additional instruction, practice, feedback, or reassessment may be needed before proficiency is demonstrated.
