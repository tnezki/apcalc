AP Calculus AB — Unit 4: Derivatives in Context

Essential Standard / Enduring Understanding

Derivatives communicate rates in context: they describe motion, connect changing quantities, support local approximation, and justify limit procedures when conditions and units are interpreted correctly.

Unit Source Alignment

Framework Alignment
  • Framework Topics / Standards: 4.1: AP Topics 4.1, 4.3; 4.2: AP Topics 4.2; 4.3: AP Topics 4.4; 4.4: AP Topics 4.5; 4.5: AP Topics 4.6, 4.7.

Section Outline

Section 4.1: Meaning of Derivatives

Learning Target: Interpret $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ in context using appropriate units, rates, and verbal meaning.

I Can Statements

  • I can interpret $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ in context using appropriate units, rates, and verbal meaning.
  • I can write context-specific interpretations of derivatives.
  • I can compare meanings of $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ in one situation.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • rate of change
  • units
  • first derivative
  • second derivative

Key Representations

  • contextual verbal descriptions
  • function/derivative notation
  • tables and graphs with units

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Giving a derivative value without rate units.
  • Describing $f'$ as the original quantity rather than its rate of change.
  • Treating the second derivative as simply another velocity-like quantity without context.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Write context-specific interpretations of derivatives.
  • Compare meanings of $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ in one situation.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Interpret $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ in context using appropriate units, rates, and verbal meaning.

Last Focus: Synthesize derivative rules, higher-order notation, procedure selection, and early motion connections to solve mixed derivative problems efficiently and accurately.

Mastery Priorities: Derivative notation and rules; graph/table interpretation; units and rate meaning; limit and continuity reasoning when relevant.

Section 4.5: Linearization and L'Hospital's Rule

Learning Target: Use local linearity for tangent-line approximation and apply L'Hospital's Rule only to appropriate indeterminate forms with required conditions.

I Can Statements

  • I can use local linearity for tangent-line approximation and apply L'Hospital's Rule only to appropriate indeterminate forms with required conditions.
  • I can estimate function values with a tangent model and discuss over/under behavior.
  • I can decide whether L'Hospital's Rule is applicable before using it.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • linearization
  • local linearity
  • tangent-line approximation
  • indeterminate form
  • L'Hospital's Rule

Key Representations

  • tangent line models
  • function and derivative values
  • limit forms
  • symbolic limits

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Using linearization far from the center without considering local accuracy.
  • Applying L'Hospital's Rule to a form that is not an appropriate indeterminate form.
  • Differentiating numerator and denominator when the rule conditions are not established.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Estimate function values with a tangent model and discuss over/under behavior.
  • Decide whether L'Hospital's Rule is applicable before using it.

Unit Themes / Throughlines

Unit Themes / Throughlines
  • Attach units and contextual meaning to derivative values.
  • Use signs and multiple representations to reason about motion.
  • Translate verbal contexts into variable relationships before differentiating.
  • Interpret related-rate answers in terms of direction and units.
  • Check conditions before using local linearity or L'Hospital's Rule.

Assessment Methods by DOK Level

DOK 1 — Foundational Evidence

Format: brief proficiency checks, direct computation or reading, notation and recognition tasks.

Purpose: Gather evidence of foundational recall, recognition, notation, and direct execution in Unit 4.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Derivative Meaning and Units Check Eligible After Section 4.1 — Identify the contextual meaning and units of $f$, $f'$, or $f''$ in direct situations. Evidence focus: Reveals whether derivative values are interpreted rather than treated as unitless numbers.
  2. Motion Quantity Recognition Eligible After Section 4.2 — Match position, velocity, acceleration, and speed to direct formulas or graph features. Evidence focus: Reveals foundational motion vocabulary and sign meaning.
  3. Related-Rate Setup Vocabulary Eligible After Section 4.3 — Identify quantities, rates, constants, and the instant of interest from a short context. Evidence focus: Reveals whether students distinguish values from rates.
  4. Linearization and Indeterminate-Form Check Eligible After Section 4.5 — Recognize tangent-line approximation components and appropriate L'Hospital forms. Evidence focus: Reveals prerequisite recognition for both procedures.
What this tells the teacher:
Whether students are demonstrating the intended DOK 1 evidence for Unit 4 without promoting routine work to a higher cognitive level.
DOK 2 — Application Evidence

Format: multi-representation application, routine method selection, applied computation, and interpretation.

Purpose: Gather evidence of routine application and translation among unit representations in Unit 4.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Context Interpretation Application Eligible After Section 4.1 — Interpret derivative values with units in standard applied settings. Evidence focus: Shows routine contextual translation.
  2. Motion Sign Analysis Eligible After Section 4.2 — Use velocity and acceleration signs to determine direction and speeding-up/slowing-down behavior. Evidence focus: Shows application of motion relationships.
  3. Related Rates Setup and Solve Eligible After Section 4.4 — Construct and solve a standard related-rates model with an explicit geometric relationship. Evidence focus: Shows routine multi-step modeling.
  4. Linearization or L'Hospital Application Eligible After Section 4.5 — Apply a tangent approximation or L'Hospital's Rule after conditions are explicitly satisfied. Evidence focus: Shows procedural application with condition awareness.

DOK 3 — Strategic / Reasoning Evidence

Format: constructed response, method comparison, critique, theorem/condition reasoning, and representation synthesis.

Purpose: Gather evidence of strategic reasoning, justification, and non-routine method selection in Unit 4.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Context Claim Critique Eligible After Section 4.1 — Evaluate a derivative interpretation for meaning, units, and consistency with the context, then repair it. Evidence focus: Shows strategic interpretation and precision.
  2. Motion Representation Synthesis Eligible After Section 4.2 — Reconcile position, velocity, and acceleration information from multiple representations in a non-routine motion scenario. Evidence focus: Shows synthesis across motion representations.
  3. Related Rates Model Choice Eligible After Section 4.4 — Compare possible constraint equations for a related-rates situation and justify the most useful model. Evidence focus: Shows strategic modeling choice.
  4. Method Conditions Analysis Eligible After Section 4.5 — Decide between algebraic limit methods and L'Hospital's Rule, or evaluate a linearization claim, when conditions are not explicit. Evidence focus: Shows condition-based method reasoning.

DOK 4 — Performance Evidence

Format: modeling performance tasks, investigations, multi-condition analysis, synthesis, and critique-and-revise work.

Purpose: Gather evidence of extended performance, modeling, transfer, synthesis, and revision in Unit 4.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Motion Model Investigation Eligible After Section 4.2 — Analyze a motion data set or simulated trajectory using position, velocity, and acceleration, then revise conclusions as representations are compared. Evidence focus: Shows extended contextual modeling.
  2. Related Rates Performance Eligible After Section 4.4 — Build, solve, and interpret a multi-condition related-rates model from a diagram or measurement scenario, checking assumptions and units. Evidence focus: Shows full performance modeling.
  3. Local Linearity Experiment Eligible After Section 4.5 — Test tangent-line approximations at increasing distances from a point and formulate evidence-based claims about local accuracy. Evidence focus: Shows investigation and model evaluation.
  4. Contextual Derivative Synthesis Eligible After Section 4.5 — Create a coherent analysis of a changing system that uses derivative meaning, rates, and approximation, then critique and revise the model. Evidence focus: Shows transfer across unit concepts.

Summative Assessment Plan

Unit 4 Summative Assessment Eligible after Section 4.5, with timing adjusted to the framework pacing and instructional needs.

Recommended format: A course-appropriate AP-style mixed assessment using selected-response and constructed-response/FRQ-style evidence, with calculator or no-calculator conditions when the relevant framework section calls for them. The plan does not prescribe a fixed paper-only length.

Major evidence priorities: contextual derivative interpretation with units; motion; related rates; linearization; and condition-aware use of L'Hospital's Rule.

Representation / reasoning / interpretation expectations: Students should work across the representations emphasized in the unit, use precise notation, justify conclusions when conditions matter, and include units or contextual interpretation when the framework makes them relevant.

Conventional summative questions should use DOK levels that naturally fit the assessed content. DOK 4 evidence is normally collected through performance-oriented modeling, investigation, design, synthesis, or transfer tasks rather than forced into ordinary test questions.

Spiral / retention statement: Include deliberate retrieval of earlier prerequisite ideas that remain active in the unit, especially notation, representation interpretation, and previously established calculus relationships; do not treat spiral work as random review.

Holistic Rubric

A — Highly Proficient

The student independently interprets derivatives in context, solves changing-quantity models, and applies approximation or limit procedures with correct conditions and units. Work is accurate, well-organized, and supported by clear reasoning. The student connects relevant representations, concepts, evidence, or models and communicates conclusions with precision and independence.

B — Proficient

The student generally demonstrates proficiency with contextual derivative interpretation with units; motion; related rates; linearization; and condition-aware use of L'Hospital's Rule. Most work is accurate, and the student can complete standard and moderately complex tasks independently. Reasoning and communication are generally clear, though they may lack some precision, flexibility, or depth.

C — Developing Proficiency

The student demonstrates developing proficiency with contextual derivative interpretation with units; motion; related rates; linearization; and condition-aware use of L'Hospital's Rule. The student can complete many routine tasks but may need support with reasoning, representations, notation, evidence, modeling, or multi-step problem solving. Work may contain errors or incomplete explanations, but there is sufficient evidence of developing understanding of the central ideas.

Not Yet — Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet demonstrate consistent proficiency with contextual derivative interpretation with units; motion; related rates; linearization; and condition-aware use of L'Hospital's Rule. Work shows major misconceptions, significant procedural or conceptual errors, weak reasoning, or difficulty connecting the representations, evidence, or models required by the unit. Additional instruction, practice, feedback, or reassessment may be needed before proficiency is demonstrated.
