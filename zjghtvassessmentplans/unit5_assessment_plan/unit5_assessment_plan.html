AP Calculus AB — Unit 5: Graph Analysis

Essential Standard / Enduring Understanding

Derivative signs, theorem conditions, and relationships among $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ allow students to analyze graph behavior and justify extrema, concavity, and optimization conclusions.

Unit Source Alignment

Framework Alignment
  • Framework Topics / Standards: 5.1: AP Topics 5.1, 5.2; 5.2: AP Topics 5.3, 5.4; 5.3: AP Topics 5.6, 5.7; 5.4: AP Topics 5.8, 5.9, 5.12; 5.5: AP Topics 5.10, 5.11.
  • Course-Specific Emphasis: College Board AP Calculus AB topic sequence supported by teacher-created warm-ups, guided notes, investigations, cooperative learning, AP Classroom practice, optional FRQ-style writing, and continual attention to justification, notation, units, and interpretation.
  • Pacing Guidance: The framework suggests approximately 12-13 instructional days for Unit 5, but sections may be compressed, extended, reordered, or supported with optional resources as needed; optional third-day work is not a required calendar.

Section Outline

Section 5.1: MVT, EVT, and Critical Points

Learning Target: Apply the Mean Value Theorem and Extreme Value Theorem with appropriate hypotheses and conclusions, and identify critical points and local or global extrema.

I Can Statements

  • I can apply the Mean Value Theorem and Extreme Value Theorem with appropriate hypotheses and conclusions, and identify critical points and local or global extrema.
  • I can verify theorem hypotheses on an interval.
  • I can use critical points and endpoints to analyze extrema.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • Mean Value Theorem
  • Extreme Value Theorem
  • critical point
  • local extremum
  • global extremum

Key Representations

  • function graphs
  • theorem hypothesis/conclusion statements
  • derivative equations
  • intervals

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Using a theorem without checking continuity or differentiability conditions.
  • Treating every critical point as an extremum.
  • Confusing local and absolute extrema.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Verify theorem hypotheses on an interval.
  • Use critical points and endpoints to analyze extrema.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Apply the Mean Value Theorem and Extreme Value Theorem with appropriate hypotheses and conclusions, and identify critical points and local or global extrema.

Last Focus: Use local linearity for tangent-line approximation and apply L'Hospital's Rule only to appropriate indeterminate forms with required conditions.

Mastery Priorities: Derivative notation and rules; graph/table interpretation; units and rate meaning; limit and continuity reasoning when relevant.

Section 5.2: Increasing, Decreasing, and First Derivative Test

Learning Target: Use the sign of the first derivative to determine intervals of increase/decrease and classify local extrema with the first derivative test.

I Can Statements

  • I can use the sign of the first derivative to determine intervals of increase/decrease and classify local extrema with the first derivative test.
  • I can build and interpret a first-derivative sign chart.
  • I can justify local extrema from derivative sign changes.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • increasing
  • decreasing
  • first derivative test
  • sign chart

Key Representations

  • sign charts for $f'$
  • graphs of $f$ and $f'$
  • interval notation

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Using the sign of $f$ instead of $f'$ to decide increase/decrease.
  • Calling a critical point an extremum without checking a sign change.
  • Using closed endpoints incorrectly in open-interval monotonicity statements.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Build and interpret a first-derivative sign chart.
  • Justify local extrema from derivative sign changes.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Use the sign of the first derivative to determine intervals of increase/decrease and classify local extrema with the first derivative test.

Last Focus: Apply the Mean Value Theorem and Extreme Value Theorem with appropriate hypotheses and conclusions, and identify critical points and local or global extrema.

Mastery Priorities: Derivative notation and rules; graph/table interpretation; units and rate meaning; limit and continuity reasoning when relevant.

Section 5.3: Concavity and Second Derivative Test

Learning Target: Use second-derivative information to determine concavity, identify inflection points, and apply the second derivative test when appropriate.

I Can Statements

  • I can use second-derivative information to determine concavity, identify inflection points, and apply the second derivative test when appropriate.
  • I can analyze concavity from $f''$.
  • I can classify extrema using a qualified second derivative test.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • concavity
  • inflection point
  • second derivative test

Key Representations

  • sign charts for $f''$
  • graphs of $f$, $f'$, and $f''$
  • interval notation

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Assuming $f''=0$ automatically gives an inflection point.
  • Confusing concave up with increasing.
  • Using the second derivative test when its conditions do not determine a result.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Analyze concavity from $f''$.
  • Classify extrema using a qualified second derivative test.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Use second-derivative information to determine concavity, identify inflection points, and apply the second derivative test when appropriate.

Last Focus: Use the sign of the first derivative to determine intervals of increase/decrease and classify local extrema with the first derivative test.

Mastery Priorities: Derivative notation and rules; graph/table interpretation; units and rate meaning; limit and continuity reasoning when relevant.

Section 5.4: Connecting f, f prime, and f double-prime

Learning Target: Connect the behavior of $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ across graphical, numerical, analytical, and implicit representations.

I Can Statements

  • I can connect the behavior of $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ across graphical, numerical, analytical, and implicit representations.
  • I can match derivative-related graphs.
  • I can predict missing behavior in one representation from another.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • derivative graph
  • second derivative
  • critical point
  • inflection point

Key Representations

  • matched graphs of $f$, $f'$, $f''$
  • tables
  • analytical expressions
  • implicit relationships

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Matching graphs by shape alone without using sign and zero relationships.
  • Confusing extrema of $f$ with extrema of $f'$.
  • Inferring concavity from the value of $f$ instead of change in $f'$.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Match derivative-related graphs.
  • Predict missing behavior in one representation from another.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Connect the behavior of $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ across graphical, numerical, analytical, and implicit representations.

Last Focus: Use second-derivative information to determine concavity, identify inflection points, and apply the second derivative test when appropriate.

Mastery Priorities: Derivative notation and rules; graph/table interpretation; units and rate meaning; limit and continuity reasoning when relevant.

Section 5.5: Optimization

Learning Target: Model optimization situations with quantities, constraints, and objective functions, then use calculus-based reasoning to identify and justify optimal values.

I Can Statements

  • I can model optimization situations with quantities, constraints, and objective functions, then use calculus-based reasoning to identify and justify optimal values.
  • I can construct an objective function from a context.
  • I can justify a maximum or minimum with calculus and domain reasoning.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • optimization
  • constraint
  • objective function
  • domain
  • absolute extremum

Key Representations

  • context diagrams
  • constraint equations
  • objective functions
  • derivative sign or critical-point analysis

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Optimizing the constraint instead of the objective.
  • Ignoring the feasible domain or endpoints.
  • Finding a critical value without showing why it gives the required optimum.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Construct an objective function from a context.
  • Justify a maximum or minimum with calculus and domain reasoning.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Model optimization situations with quantities, constraints, and objective functions, then use calculus-based reasoning to identify and justify optimal values.

Last Focus: Connect the behavior of $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ across graphical, numerical, analytical, and implicit representations.

Mastery Priorities: Derivative notation and rules; graph/table interpretation; units and rate meaning; limit and continuity reasoning when relevant.

Unit Themes / Throughlines

Unit Themes / Throughlines
  • Use theorem hypotheses explicitly before drawing conclusions.
  • Connect derivative signs and zeros to graph behavior.
  • Distinguish increasing/decreasing behavior from concavity.
  • Move among $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ representations.
  • Model optimization with constraints, feasible domains, and justified extrema.

Assessment Methods by DOK Level

DOK 1 — Foundational Evidence

Format: brief proficiency checks, direct computation or reading, notation and recognition tasks.

Purpose: Gather evidence of foundational recall, recognition, notation, and direct execution in Unit 5.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Theorem Condition Check Eligible After Section 5.1 — Identify required hypotheses or direct conclusions for MVT and EVT in concise cases. Evidence focus: Reveals theorem-language fluency.
  2. First-Derivative Sign Recognition Eligible After Section 5.2 — Read a sign chart and identify intervals of increase/decrease or direct extrema. Evidence focus: Reveals foundational sign interpretation.
  3. Concavity Recognition Eligible After Section 5.3 — Identify concavity or likely inflection locations from direct second-derivative information. Evidence focus: Reveals basic second-derivative interpretation.
  4. f-f'-f'' Feature Match Eligible After Section 5.4 — Match direct features such as zeros, signs, extrema, and concavity across related graphs. Evidence focus: Reveals foundational representation connections.
What this tells the teacher:
Whether students are demonstrating the intended DOK 1 evidence for Unit 5 without promoting routine work to a higher cognitive level.
DOK 2 — Application Evidence

Format: multi-representation application, routine method selection, applied computation, and interpretation.

Purpose: Gather evidence of routine application and translation among unit representations in Unit 5.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Theorem Application Eligible After Section 5.1 — Verify stated hypotheses and apply MVT or EVT in a standard interval setting. Evidence focus: Shows routine theorem application.
  2. First Derivative Test Eligible After Section 5.2 — Construct a sign chart and classify local extrema in a routine problem. Evidence focus: Shows application of derivative sign reasoning.
  3. Second Derivative Analysis Eligible After Section 5.3 — Determine concavity, inflection points, or an extremum classification using second-derivative information. Evidence focus: Shows routine graph analysis.
  4. Optimization Setup and Solve Eligible After Section 5.5 — Build and solve a standard optimization model with an explicit constraint. Evidence focus: Shows routine modeling and extremum analysis.
What this tells the teacher:
Whether students are demonstrating the intended DOK 2 evidence for Unit 5 without promoting routine work to a higher cognitive level.
DOK 3 — Strategic / Reasoning Evidence

Format: constructed response, method comparison, critique, theorem/condition reasoning, and representation synthesis.

Purpose: Gather evidence of strategic reasoning, justification, and non-routine method selection in Unit 5.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Theorem Claim Critique Eligible After Section 5.1 — Evaluate a theorem-based conclusion when one or more hypotheses are uncertain, and justify whether the conclusion is guaranteed. Evidence focus: Shows theorem-condition reasoning.
  2. Graph Analysis Strategy Eligible After Section 5.4 — Synthesize $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ information to resolve a non-routine graph-behavior question. Evidence focus: Shows representation synthesis.
  3. Extrema Evidence Comparison Eligible After Section 5.3 — Compare first- and second-derivative evidence for an extremum and justify which evidence is decisive. Evidence focus: Shows strategic method comparison.
  4. Optimization Model Defense Eligible After Section 5.5 — Compare two plausible objective/constraint models and defend the one that correctly represents the optimization context. Evidence focus: Shows strategic modeling choice.
What this tells the teacher:
Whether students are demonstrating the intended DOK 3 evidence for Unit 5 without promoting routine work to a higher cognitive level.
DOK 4 — Performance Evidence

Format: modeling performance tasks, investigations, multi-condition analysis, synthesis, and critique-and-revise work.

Purpose: Gather evidence of extended performance, modeling, transfer, synthesis, and revision in Unit 5.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Function Behavior Investigation Eligible After Section 5.4 — Analyze a function family across graphical and derivative representations, formulate behavior rules, and revise them against counterexamples. Evidence focus: Shows extended synthesis and revision.
  2. Theorem Performance Task Eligible After Section 5.1 — Evaluate several interval scenarios, test theorem hypotheses, and construct or revise examples to show when conclusions do or do not follow. Evidence focus: Shows extended theorem reasoning.
  3. Optimization Design Task Eligible After Section 5.5 — Design and solve a constrained optimization model, test boundary cases, and revise assumptions or the objective as needed. Evidence focus: Shows performance-level modeling.
  4. Graph Reconstruction Performance Eligible After Section 5.4 — Construct a plausible graph of $f$ from multi-condition information about $f'$ and $f''$, test consistency, and revise the graph. Evidence focus: Shows extended transfer among derivative representations.

Summative Assessment Plan

Unit 5 Summative Assessment Eligible after Section 5.5, with timing adjusted to the framework pacing and instructional needs.

Recommended format: A course-appropriate AP-style mixed assessment using selected-response and constructed-response/FRQ-style evidence, with calculator or no-calculator conditions when the relevant framework section calls for them. The plan does not prescribe a fixed paper-only length.

Major evidence priorities: MVT/EVT conditions; first- and second-derivative graph analysis; connections among $f$, $f'$, and $f''$; and optimization.

Representation / reasoning / interpretation expectations: Students should work across the representations emphasized in the unit, use precise notation, justify conclusions when conditions matter, and include units or contextual interpretation when the framework makes them relevant.

Conventional summative questions should use DOK levels that naturally fit the assessed content. DOK 4 evidence is normally collected through performance-oriented modeling, investigation, design, synthesis, or transfer tasks rather than forced into ordinary test questions.

Spiral / retention statement: Include deliberate retrieval of earlier prerequisite ideas that remain active in the unit, especially notation, representation interpretation, and previously established calculus relationships; do not treat spiral work as random review.

Holistic Rubric

A — Highly Proficient

The student independently uses theorem conditions and derivative evidence to analyze function behavior and justify extrema, concavity, and optimization conclusions. Work is accurate, well-organized, and supported by clear reasoning. The student connects relevant representations, concepts, evidence, or models and communicates conclusions with precision and independence.

B — Proficient

The student generally demonstrates proficiency with MVT/EVT conditions; first- and second-derivative graph analysis; connections among $f$, $f'$, and $f''$; and optimization. Most work is accurate, and the student can complete standard and moderately complex tasks independently. Reasoning and communication are generally clear, though they may lack some precision, flexibility, or depth.

C — Developing Proficiency

The student demonstrates developing proficiency with MVT/EVT conditions; first- and second-derivative graph analysis; connections among $f$, $f'$, and $f''$; and optimization. The student can complete many routine tasks but may need support with reasoning, representations, notation, evidence, modeling, or multi-step problem solving. Work may contain errors or incomplete explanations, but there is sufficient evidence of developing understanding of the central ideas.

Not Yet — Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet demonstrate consistent proficiency with MVT/EVT conditions; first- and second-derivative graph analysis; connections among $f$, $f'$, and $f''$; and optimization. Work shows major misconceptions, significant procedural or conceptual errors, weak reasoning, or difficulty connecting the representations, evidence, or models required by the unit. Additional instruction, practice, feedback, or reassessment may be needed before proficiency is demonstrated.
