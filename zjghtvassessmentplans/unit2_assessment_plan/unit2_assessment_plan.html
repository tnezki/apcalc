AP Calculus AB — Unit 2: Derivative Basics

Essential Standard / Enduring Understanding

The derivative is a limit that measures instantaneous change and tangent slope, and derivative rules provide efficient ways to compute that change while preserving meaning, notation, and differentiability conditions.

Section Outline

Section 2.1: Average to Instant Rate

Learning Target: Connect average rate of change and secant slopes to instantaneous rate of change and tangent slope estimates.

I Can Statements

  • I can connect average rate of change and secant slopes to instantaneous rate of change and tangent slope estimates.
  • I can estimate tangent slope from shrinking intervals.
  • I can interpret average and instantaneous rates in a shared context.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • average rate of change
  • instantaneous rate of change
  • secant line
  • tangent line
  • difference quotient

Key Representations

  • secant and tangent line graphs
  • difference quotient expressions
  • tables of shrinking intervals

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Using a single secant slope as the exact instantaneous rate.
  • Confusing change in output with average rate of change.
  • Losing the interval width in a difference quotient.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Estimate tangent slope from shrinking intervals.
  • Interpret average and instantaneous rates in a shared context.

Spiral Planning

Current Focus: Connect average rate of change and secant slopes to instantaneous rate of change and tangent slope estimates.

Last Focus: Interpret infinite limits and limits at infinity to identify asymptotic behavior, and apply the Intermediate Value Theorem when its hypotheses are satisfied.

Mastery Priorities: Function notation and graph interpretation; algebraic manipulation; limit meaning and notation; rate-of-change reasoning.

Section 2.3: Differentiability

Learning Target: Determine where a function is differentiable and connect differentiability to continuity, corners, cusps, vertical tangents, and discontinuities.

  • I can classify points of nondifferentiability from graphs.
  • I can justify why a function can be continuous but not differentiable at a point.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • differentiability
  • continuity
  • corner
  • cusp
  • vertical tangent

Key Representations

  • graphs with nondifferentiable points
  • local tangent behavior
  • continuity/differentiability implication statements

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Assuming continuity guarantees differentiability.
  • Treating a vertical tangent as an ordinary finite derivative.
  • Missing nondifferentiability at corners or cusps on an otherwise continuous graph.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Classify points of nondifferentiability from graphs.
  • Justify why a function can be continuous but not differentiable at a point.

  • quotient rule
  • tangent line

Key Representations

  • tangent line equations
  • symbolic derivative work

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Using the product rule as the product of derivatives.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

Assessment Methods by DOK Level

DOK 1 — Foundational Evidence

Format: brief proficiency checks, direct computation or reading, notation and recognition tasks.

Purpose: Gather evidence of foundational recall, recognition, notation, and direct execution in Unit 2.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Rate and Slope Vocabulary Check Eligible After Section 2.1 — Recognize secant, tangent, average-rate, and instantaneous-rate quantities in direct representations. Evidence focus: Reveals whether the conceptual language of rates is secure.
  2. Derivative Notation Check Eligible After Section 2.2 — Read and match common derivative notation with the quantity it represents. Evidence focus: Reveals notation fluency.
  3. Differentiability Feature ID Eligible After Section 2.3 — Identify corners, cusps, vertical tangents, and discontinuities that affect differentiability. Evidence focus: Reveals whether students recognize key graphical features.
  4. Basic Derivative Rule Fluency Eligible After Section 2.4 — Differentiate routine expressions using an explicitly appropriate basic rule. Evidence focus: Reveals foundational procedural accuracy.
What this tells the teacher:
Whether students are demonstrating the intended DOK 1 evidence for Unit 2 without promoting routine work to a higher cognitive level.
DOK 2 — Application Evidence

Format: multi-representation application, routine method selection, applied computation, and interpretation.

Purpose: Gather evidence of routine application and translation among unit representations in Unit 2.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Secant-to-Tangent Estimate Eligible After Section 2.1 — Use shrinking intervals or a table to estimate an instantaneous rate and connect it to tangent slope. Evidence focus: Shows routine translation from average to instantaneous change.
  2. Derivative Definition Application Eligible After Section 2.2 — Evaluate or interpret a derivative from its limit definition in a standard case. Evidence focus: Shows understanding of the definition as a usable representation.
  3. Differentiability Classification Eligible After Section 2.3 — Determine differentiability across a graph and explain the role of continuity and local features. Evidence focus: Shows application of conditions.
  4. Mixed Rule Selection Eligible After Section 2.5 — Select and apply power, product, quotient, or core-function rules in mixed routine expressions. Evidence focus: Shows rule selection and execution.
What this tells the teacher:
Whether students are demonstrating the intended DOK 2 evidence for Unit 2 without promoting routine work to a higher cognitive level.
DOK 3 — Strategic / Reasoning Evidence

Format: constructed response, method comparison, critique, theorem/condition reasoning, and representation synthesis.

Purpose: Gather evidence of strategic reasoning, justification, and non-routine method selection in Unit 2.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Definition vs Rule Strategy Eligible After Section 2.5 — Compare definition-based and rule-based derivative approaches in a case where efficiency and interpretive value differ. Evidence focus: Shows strategic choice and justification.
  2. Differentiability Argument Critique Eligible After Section 2.3 — Critique an argument that incorrectly equates continuity and differentiability, then repair it with graphical or symbolic evidence. Evidence focus: Shows condition-based reasoning.
  3. Derivative Representation Synthesis Eligible After Section 2.2 — Reconcile derivative information from a graph, table, tangent line, and notation when one source is incomplete. Evidence focus: Shows synthesis across representations.
  4. Rule Selection and Structure Defense Eligible After Section 2.5 — Defend a non-obvious rule sequence for a mixed expression and explain why a competing path fails or is inefficient. Evidence focus: Shows strategic structure analysis.
What this tells the teacher:
Whether students are demonstrating the intended DOK 3 evidence for Unit 2 without promoting routine work to a higher cognitive level.
DOK 4 — Performance Evidence

Format: modeling performance tasks, investigations, multi-condition analysis, synthesis, and critique-and-revise work.

Purpose: Gather evidence of extended performance, modeling, transfer, synthesis, and revision in Unit 2.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Instantaneous Rate Investigation Eligible After Section 2.2 — Use multiple secant intervals to investigate a tangent slope, compare with a derivative calculation, and revise estimates or assumptions. Evidence focus: Shows extended evidence-based convergence reasoning.
  2. Differentiability Case Study Eligible After Section 2.3 — Create or analyze a family of graphs with continuity and differentiability variations, test claims, and refine the classification criteria. Evidence focus: Shows performance-level conceptual synthesis.

Summative Assessment Plan

Unit 2 Summative Assessment Eligible after Section 2.5, with timing adjusted to the framework pacing and instructional needs.

Recommended format: A course-appropriate AP-style mixed assessment using selected-response and constructed-response/FRQ-style evidence, with calculator or no-calculator conditions when the relevant framework section calls for them. The plan does not prescribe a fixed paper-only length.

Major evidence priorities: derivative meaning and notation; differentiability; basic, product, and quotient rules; and tangent-line applications.

Representation / reasoning / interpretation expectations: Students should work across the representations emphasized in the unit, use precise notation, justify conclusions when conditions matter, and include units or contextual interpretation when the framework makes them relevant.

Conventional summative questions should use DOK levels that naturally fit the assessed content. DOK 4 evidence is normally collected through performance-oriented modeling, investigation, design, synthesis, or transfer tasks rather than forced into ordinary test questions.

Spiral / retention statement: Include deliberate retrieval of earlier prerequisite ideas that remain active in the unit, especially notation, representation interpretation, and previously established calculus relationships; do not treat spiral work as random review.

Holistic Rubric

A — Highly Proficient

The student independently connects the derivative definition, differentiability, and derivative rules to accurate tangent and rate interpretations. Work is accurate, well-organized, and supported by clear reasoning. The student connects relevant representations, concepts, evidence, or models and communicates conclusions with precision and independence.

B — Proficient

The student generally demonstrates proficiency with derivative meaning and notation; differentiability; basic, product, and quotient rules; and tangent-line applications. Most work is accurate, and the student can complete standard and moderately complex tasks independently. Reasoning and communication are generally clear, though they may lack some precision, flexibility, or depth.

C — Developing Proficiency

The student demonstrates developing proficiency with derivative meaning and notation; differentiability; basic, product, and quotient rules; and tangent-line applications. The student can complete many routine tasks but may need support with reasoning, representations, notation, evidence, modeling, or multi-step problem solving. Work may contain errors or incomplete explanations, but there is sufficient evidence of developing understanding of the central ideas.

Not Yet — Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet demonstrate consistent proficiency with derivative meaning and notation; differentiability; basic, product, and quotient rules; and tangent-line applications. Work shows major misconceptions, significant procedural or conceptual errors, weak reasoning, or difficulty connecting the representations, evidence, or models required by the unit. Additional instruction, practice, feedback, or reassessment may be needed before proficiency is demonstrated.
