Limits Involving Infinity

Infinity describes a direction of behavior, not a number to substitute.

What you'll learn about

Learning Target: Analyze end behavior and infinite limits and connect them to asymptotes.

  • I can evaluate finite limits as $x\to\infty$ and $x\to-\infty$.
  • I can use end-behavior models to identify horizontal or slant asymptotes.
  • I can interpret one-sided infinite behavior and identify vertical asymptotes.
...and why

Limits involving infinity describe what a function does far beyond the visible graph and near inputs where the function grows without bound.

Section Introduction

Two different ways infinity enters a limit

There are two distinct ideas in this section. We can let the input move farther and farther to the right or left and ask what the function approaches, or we can let the input approach a fixed value while the output grows without bound.

For rational functions, the highest-power terms control long-run behavior. End-behavior models make that comparison visible and help identify horizontal or slant asymptotes.

Near a vertical asymptote, one-sided limits matter because the function may increase or decrease without bound differently on the two sides.

Keep the two roles of infinity separate: sometimes the input goes to infinity; other times the output does.

What's To Come

PREVIEW / DECOMPOSE. Use the connected parts to see where this section is headed.

Consider $r(x)=\dfrac{2x+1}{x-3}$. Use the structure of the expression before reaching for a calculator.

Part A

As $x\to\infty$, which terms control the fraction? Predict the limiting value.

Teacher support
  • Answer: As $x\to\infty$, the leading terms $2x$ and $x$ control the ratio, so the limit is $2$.
  • Reasoning: Dividing numerator and denominator by $x$ gives $\frac{2+1/x}{1-3/x}\to2$.
  • Teaching move / listen for: Have students make the dominant-term prediction before using algebra.

Part B

Make the same prediction as $x\to-\infty$. Should the two end-behavior limits match?

Teacher support
  • Answer: As $x\to-\infty$, the same leading-term ratio gives $2$, so the two end-behavior limits match.
  • Reasoning: The sign of $x$ changes, but both numerator and denominator are dominated by first-degree terms with the same factor of $x$.
  • Teaching move / listen for: Ask why “negative infinity” does not automatically produce a negative limit.

Part C

What happens to the denominator as $x\to3^-$ and as $x\to3^+$? Predict the sign of $r(x)$ on each side.

Teacher support
  • Answer: As $x\to3^-$, the denominator is a small negative number while the numerator is positive, so $r(x)\to-\infty$. As $x\to3^+$, $r(x)\to+\infty$.
  • Reasoning: Sign analysis around the zero of the denominator determines the one-sided infinite behavior.
  • Teaching move / listen for: Require a sign argument rather than relying only on a graphing calculator.

Part D

From your predictions, identify the horizontal and vertical asymptotes you expect to see on a graph.

Teacher support
  • Answer: The expected horizontal asymptote is $y=2$ and the vertical asymptote is $x=3$.
  • Reasoning: Finite end-behavior limits produce horizontal asymptotes; unbounded one-sided behavior at a finite input produces a vertical asymptote.
  • Teaching move / listen for: Connect each asymptote to its corresponding limit statement.

Example 1 · Finite limits as x approaches infinity

Investigate both end-behavior limits for $f(x)=\frac1x$:

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x).$$
Teacher support
  • Answer: Both $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac1x$ and $\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac1x$ equal $0$. The graph has horizontal asymptote $y=0$.
  • Reasoning: The denominator grows without bound in magnitude while the numerator remains fixed.
  • Teaching move / listen for: Stress that the function approaches $0$ without becoming $0$.

Example 2 · End behavior of a rational function

Investigate both end-behavior limits for

$$f(x)=\frac{2x-1}{x+3}:$$ $$\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x).$$
Teacher support
  • Answer: Both end-behavior limits equal $2$, so $y=2$ is the horizontal asymptote.
  • Reasoning: Divide numerator and denominator by $x$: $\frac{2-1/x}{1+3/x}\to2$.
  • Teaching move / listen for: Use this as the prototype for equal-degree rational functions.
DefinitionHorizontal Asymptote

The line $y=b$ is a horizontal asymptote of the graph of a function $y=f(x)$ if either

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=b \qquad\text{or}\qquad \lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=b.$$
Theorem 5Properties of Limits as $x\to\pm\infty$

If $L$, $M$, and $k$ are real numbers and $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}f(x)=L$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}g(x)=M$, then:

  1. Sum Rule: $\displaystyle \lim_{x\to\pm\infty}[f(x)+g(x)]=L+M$
  2. Difference Rule: $\displaystyle \lim_{x\to\pm\infty}[f(x)-g(x)]=L-M$
  3. Product Rule: $\displaystyle \lim_{x\to\pm\infty}[f(x)g(x)]=LM$
  4. Constant Multiple Rule: $\displaystyle \lim_{x\to\pm\infty}[k f(x)]=kL$
  5. Quotient Rule: $\displaystyle \lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=\frac{L}{M}$, provided $M\ne0$
  6. Power Rule: If $r$ and $s$ are integers with $s\ne0$, then $\displaystyle \lim_{x\to\pm\infty}[f(x)]^{r/s}=L^{r/s}$, provided $L^{r/s}$ is a real number.
DefinitionEnd Behavior Model

The function $g$ is

  1. a right end behavior model for $f$ if and only if $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=1$;
  2. a left end behavior model for $f$ if and only if $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=1$.

Example 3 · End behavior models and asymptotes

For each function:

  1. write the end-behavior model;
  2. evaluate the limit;
  3. determine whether a horizontal asymptote exists and, if so, give its equation;
  4. determine whether a slant (oblique) asymptote exists and, if so, give its equation.
  1. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{2x+5}{3x^2-6x+1}$
  2. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{2x^2-3x+5}{x^2+1}$
  3. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x^4+x^3+9}{3x-3}$
Teacher support
  • Answer: 1. $\frac{2x+5}{3x^2-6x+1}$: a leading-term model is $\frac{2}{3x}$; the limit is $0$; horizontal asymptote $y=0$; no slant asymptote. 2. $\frac{2x^2-3x+5}{x^2+1}$: model $2$; limit $2$; horizontal asymptote $y=2$; no slant asymptote. 3. $\frac{x^4+x^3+9}{3x-3}$: a leading-term model is $\frac13x^3$ and the function is unbounded; no horizontal or linear slant asymptote. Long division gives the refined polynomial asymptote $y=\frac13x^3+\frac23x^2+\frac23x+\frac23$.
  • Reasoning: Compare polynomial degrees first, then refine with division when the numerator degree exceeds the denominator degree.
  • Teaching move / listen for: Distinguish a horizontal asymptote, a linear slant asymptote, and a higher-degree polynomial end-behavior model.

Example 4 · A function with more than one horizontal asymptote

Investigate

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5+2^x}{3-2^x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{5+2^x}{3-2^x}.$$
Teacher support
  • Answer: $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5+2^x}{3-2^x}=-1$ and $\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{5+2^x}{3-2^x}=\frac53$. The graph therefore has different horizontal asymptotes on its right and left ends: $y=-1$ and $y=\frac53$.
  • Reasoning: As $x\to\infty$, $2^x$ dominates; as $x\to-\infty$, $2^x\to0$.
  • Teaching move / listen for: This is a useful counterexample to the misconception that a function can have only one horizontal asymptote.

Example 5 · A bounded numerator over an unbounded denominator

In the previous section, we proved $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1$. Investigate

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}.$$
Teacher support
  • Answer: $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}=0$.
  • Reasoning: Because $-1\le\sin x\le1$ and $x\gt 0$ for sufficiently large $x$, $-1/x\le\sin x/x\le1/x$; both bounds approach $0$.
  • Teaching move / listen for: Reconnect this to the Squeeze Theorem from 2.1 and emphasize bounded numerator versus unbounded denominator.

Example 6 · Infinite one-sided limits

Investigate

$$\lim_{x\to0^+}\frac1x\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{x\to0^-}\frac1x.$$
Teacher support
  • Answer: $\lim_{x\to0^+}\frac1x=+\infty$ and $\lim_{x\to0^-}\frac1x=-\infty$.
  • Reasoning: The magnitude grows without bound while the sign is determined by the side from which $x$ approaches zero.
  • Teaching move / listen for: Have students pair each one-sided statement with the corresponding branch of the graph.
DefinitionVertical Asymptote

The line $x=a$ is a vertical asymptote of the graph of a function $y=f(x)$ if a one-sided limit at $a$ is unbounded; that is, if at least one of

$$\lim_{x\to a^+}f(x) \qquad\text{or}\qquad \lim_{x\to a^-}f(x)$$

is $+\infty$ or $-\infty$.

Example 7 · Vertical asymptotes and one-sided behavior

For each function, find all vertical asymptotes and describe the behavior of the function to the left and right of each asymptote.

  1. $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x^2-1}{2x+4}$
  2. $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1-x}{2x^2-5x-3}$
  3. $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x-2}{3x^2-5x-2}$
Teacher support
  • Answer: 1. $\frac{x^2-1}{2x+4}$ has vertical asymptote $x=-2$; left side $\to-\infty$, right side $\to+\infty$. 2. $\frac{1-x}{2x^2-5x-3}$ has vertical asymptotes $x=-\frac12$ and $x=3$; at $-\frac12$: left $\to+\infty$, right $\to-\infty$; at $3$: left $\to+\infty$, right $\to-\infty$. 3. $\frac{x-2}{3x^2-5x-2}=\frac1{3x+1}$ for $x\ne2$; the only vertical asymptote is $x=-\frac13$ with left $\to-\infty$ and right $\to+\infty$; $x=2$ is a removable hole, not a vertical asymptote.
  • Reasoning: Factor denominators, cancel common factors before classifying discontinuities, then use sign analysis on each side of each remaining zero.
  • Teaching move / listen for: This is a high-value place to ask “hole or asymptote?” before doing any numerical work.
