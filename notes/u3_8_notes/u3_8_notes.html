TheoremDerivative of an Inverse Function

If $f$ is differentiable and $f'(q)\ne0$, and $f(q)=p$, then

$$ (f^{-1})'(p)=\frac{1}{f'(q)}.$$

The point $(q,p)$ on $f$ becomes $(p,q)$ on $f^{-1}$.

NotationDo Not Confuse

$\sin^{-1}x$ means inverse sine (arcsine), not $1/\sin x$. Reciprocal sine is $\csc x$.
