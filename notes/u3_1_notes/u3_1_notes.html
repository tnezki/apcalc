Section Introduction

Derivative of a Function

In the previous chapter, tangent-line slopes came from limits of secant-line slopes. In this section that same limit becomes a new function: the derivative.

The derivative can be viewed numerically, graphically, and algebraically. Every representation is telling us the same thing: how fast the original function is changing at an instant.

What's To Come

PREVIEW. Every example below is chosen to move from meaning to efficient calculation and then back to interpretation.

DefinitionDerivative as a Function

The derivative of $f$ with respect to $x$ is

$$f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h},$$

provided the limit exists. Where this limit exists, $f$ is differentiable.

DefinitionAlternate Definition at $x=c$

At a fixed input $c$,

$$f'(c)=\lim_{x\to c}\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c},$$

provided the limit exists. This form is especially useful when comparing left- and right-hand behavior.

ConnectionReading $f$ and $f'$ Together
  • $f'(x)>0$ where $f$ is increasing.
  • $f'(x)<0$ where $f$ is decreasing.
  • $f'(x)=0$ where $f$ has a horizontal tangent.
  • A graph of $f'$ describes the slopes of $f$, not the heights of $f$.

Example 1 · Build the derivative from the definition

Use the limit definition to find the derivative of $f(x)=x^3$.

Example 2 · Derivative at one point

Use the alternate definition to find $f'(2)$ for $f(x)=x^2+3x$.

Example 3 · Read the derivative from a graph

A smooth function has slopes that are negative, then zero, then positive as $x$ moves left to right. Sketch a possible graph of $f'$ and identify where $f$ has a local minimum.

Example 4 · Reconstruct $f$ from $f'$

Suppose $f'(x)=-1$ for $x<1$ and $f'(x)=2$ for $x>1$, with $f(0)=0$ and $f$ continuous. Sketch a possible graph of $f$.

Example 5 · One-sided derivatives

For $f(x)=\begin{cases}x^2,&x<0\\2x,&x\ge0\end{cases}$, find the left-hand and right-hand derivatives at $x=0$. Is $f$ differentiable there?
