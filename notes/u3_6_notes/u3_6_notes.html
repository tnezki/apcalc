Section Introduction

The Chain Rule

A composition such as $\sin(x^2-4)$ cannot be differentiated by treating the inside as just $x$. The change of the outside depends on how fast the inside is changing.

The Chain Rule multiplies those two rates.

What's To Come

PREVIEW. Every example below is chosen to move from meaning to efficient calculation and then back to interpretation.

TheoremChain Rule

If $y=f(u)$ and $u=g(x)$, then

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy}{du}\frac{du}{dx}.$$

Equivalently,

$$\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x))=f'(g(x))g'(x).$$
StrategyOutside-Inside Rule
  1. Identify the inside expression.
  2. Differentiate the outside function while leaving the inside unchanged.
  3. Multiply by the derivative of the inside.
StrategyRepeated and Combined Rules

Nested compositions may require the Chain Rule more than once. Products and quotients can contain composite factors, so several rules may be needed in one problem.

Example 1 · Simple composition

Differentiate $y=(3x^2+1)^5$ and name the inside and outside functions.

Example 2 · Trig composition

Differentiate $h(x)=\sin(2x^3-1)$.

Example 3 · Repeated Chain Rule

Differentiate $y=\left(1+\sin(x^2)\right)^4$.

Example 4 · Product + Chain

Differentiate $k(x)=(x^2+1)^2(x-3)^4$.

Example 5 · Function values

Suppose $g(5)=-3$, $g'(5)=6$, $h(5)=3$, and $h'(5)=-2$. Find $f'(5)$ if $f(x)=g(h(x))$, when possible.
