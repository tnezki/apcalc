Section Introduction

Differentiability

A graph can be continuous and still fail to have a derivative. The issue is not whether the graph is connected; it is whether the tangent slope settles to one finite value from both sides.

Four common warning signs are corners, cusps, vertical tangents, and discontinuities.

TheoremDifferentiability Implies Continuity

If $f$ is differentiable at $x=c$, then $f$ is continuous at $x=c$.

The converse is false: a function can be continuous and still have a corner, cusp, or vertical tangent.

TestHow to Check Differentiability at $x=c$
  1. Make sure $f(c)$ exists.
  2. Check that $f$ is continuous at $c$.
  3. Compare the left-hand and right-hand derivatives.
  4. The two derivative limits must exist, be finite, and be equal.
WarningWhere Derivatives Fail
  • Corner: finite one-sided slopes do not match.
  • Cusp: slopes blow up with opposite signs.
  • Vertical tangent: slopes blow up in the same direction.
  • Discontinuity: differentiability is impossible.

Example 1 · Corner
