AP Calculus AB · First 4 Days

Course Overview · Materials + Workflow

A practical student-facing guide to the opening course resource system.

Purpose: Know what kind of resource you are opening, how to use it, and how to keep your work organized so you can find it again.

Welcome / Learning Target

Projected at the start of class. It tells you the day’s focus and what you should be able to do.

Warm-Up

Short retrieval of familiar skills. Start promptly and show enough work to review later.

Notes

Short teaching + sense-making + Example → You Try It. Annotate graphs and keep a readable record of reasoning in Notability.

Practice

A learning resource, not just a score. Draw on graphs, show secant calculations, label target points, and build rectangles visibly.

Investigation / Activity

Explore, test, compare, explain, or collaborate. The participation structure may change, but the mathematics still needs evidence.

Exit Ticket

A short evidence pulse. Keep it simple and focused on what you can show at the end of class.

Notability workflow

  1. Open the exact resource named on the agenda or First 4 Days index.
  2. Place the working copy in the course location your teacher has directed you to use.
  3. Write on the page: label points, draw secants/tangents/rectangles, and keep calculations near the representation.
  4. Use a filename/folder system that lets you reopen the work later.

Calculus communication habits

  • Show the coordinates of important points.
  • Use $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$ consistently for slope calculations.
  • When you write a tangent-line estimate, show both the point and the estimated slope.
  • For LRAM, show the interval, $\Delta x$, the left heights, and the sum.
  • Include units when the quantities have units.
  • Use a short sentence when a prompt asks what a result means.
Finding missed material: Start with the course site / First 4 Days index and the resources your teacher has posted. This overview does not create a separate absence policy.
AP-style work: Some tasks will ask you to unpack notation, justify a choice, or connect representations. “I know the answer” is different from “I can communicate why it makes sense.”
