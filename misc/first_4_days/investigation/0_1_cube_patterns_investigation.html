Guess It, Test It, Draw It

About 30 minutes. Use Desmos and Notability to estimate tangent lines and left-rectangle area.

Names:

Part A — Guess the Tangent Line

Desmos: Use the ordinary Desmos calculator. No class code is needed. Graph the function and your guessed line on the same axes.

A1. Quadratic

Graph $f(x)=x^2$. Target: $x=1$.

  1. Find the target point $(x_1,y_1)$ exactly.
  2. Estimate the tangent slope $m$ by looking at nearby secants or by visual guess/check.
  3. Enter $y=m(x-x_1)+y_1$.
  4. Adjust $m$ until your line appears tangent. Record your best estimate and explain what visual evidence convinced you.

A2. Cubic

Graph $g(x)=0.35x^3-x+3$. Target: $x=0$.

  1. Record the target point.
  2. Guess and check a tangent slope.
  3. Write the line in point-slope form.
  4. Describe what happened when your slope was too large or too small.

A3. Your Check

Choose one nearby $x$-value on either graph, compute a secant slope to the target point, and compare it with your visual tangent estimate.

Part B — Build LRAM on the Graph

Notability: Draw directly on the graphs. Use only LEFT rectangles.

B1. Quadratic

For $f(x)=\tfrac12x^2+1$ on $[0,4]$, use 5 left rectangles.

  1. Find $\Delta x$.
  2. Mark the five left endpoints.
  3. Draw all five rectangles.
  4. Estimate the five heights from the graph and calculate the LRAM.
  5. Does the picture suggest an overestimate or underestimate? Explain visually.

B2. Cubic

For $h(x)=0.25x^3-0.5x^2+2$ on $[0,4]$, use 8 left rectangles.

  1. Find $\Delta x$.
  2. Draw the rectangles directly on the graph.
  3. Record enough heights to calculate a reasonable graphical estimate.
  4. Circle one rectangle whose height was hard to read and explain how you estimated it.

B3. Compare the Two Big Ideas

Complete the sentence: A tangent estimate uses nearby points to measure ________, while LRAM uses rectangles to measure ________.
