AP Calculus AB — Unit 7: Differential Equations

Essential Standard / Enduring Understanding

Differential equations model how quantities change; slope fields visualize local behavior, separation of variables produces solution families, and initial conditions or exponential structures determine contextual particular solutions.

Section 7.1: Modeling Differential Equations

AP Topics: 7.1, 7.2

Learning Target: Translate verbal rate relationships into differential equations and verify whether proposed functions satisfy a differential equation.

I Can Statements

  • I can translate verbal rate relationships into differential equations and verify whether proposed functions satisfy a differential equation.
  • I can model a verbal growth or change statement with a differential equation.
  • I can verify a proposed solution by substitution.

Key Vocabulary / Notation

  • differential equation
  • solution
  • rate relationship
  • verification

Key Representations

  • verbal statements
  • differential equations
  • candidate solution functions

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Writing an algebraic equation instead of a rate equation.
  • Checking a candidate function value without checking its derivative relationship.
  • Ignoring variable dependence in a contextual differential equation.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Model a verbal growth or change statement with a differential equation.
  • Verify a proposed solution by substitution.

Recurring Practice Opportunities

  • Interpretation: Connect the computed or inferred quantity to the variables/context and include units or sign meaning when the source situation provides them.
  • Modeling: Translate between the stated situation and a mathematical model/diagram, preserving the meaning of variables, dimensions, and relationships.
  • Notation / communication: Communicate the reasoning with precise AP Calculus notation and complete mathematical statements.

  • Match a slope field to a differential equation.

Section 7.4: Particular Solutions

Learning Target: Use initial conditions to determine constants and produce particular solutions to separable differential equations.

I Can Statements

  • I can use initial conditions to determine constants and produce particular solutions to separable differential equations.
  • I can determine a particular solution from an initial condition.
  • I can connect an initial point to one member of a solution family.

  • initial condition
  • particular solution
  • general solution
  • constant of integration

Key Representations

  • general solution families
  • initial-condition points
  • particular solution graphs/equations

Common Misconceptions / Failure Points

  • Substituting the initial condition before obtaining a valid general relationship.
  • Solving for the constant incorrectly from the initial condition.
  • Leaving a general solution when a particular solution is requested.

Modeling / Application Opportunities

  • Determine a particular solution from an initial condition.
  • Connect an initial point to one member of a solution family.

Recurring Practice Opportunities

  • Justification / conditions: Identify the mathematical condition or evidence that supports the conclusion rather than naming a rule or theorem without support.
  • Modeling: Translate between the stated situation and a mathematical model/diagram, preserving the meaning of variables, dimensions, and relationships.
  • Interpretation: State what the result means in relation to the mathematical objects and representation being used.

Selected / Constructed / Performance Balance: Use a balanced mix of brief AP-style selected-response evidence and constructed-response evidence that makes reasoning, notation, interpretation, or justification visible. Calculator / Reference Rules: Include calculator-permitted and non-calculator reasoning where the unit/framework naturally supports those modes. Assessment Time: The framework suggests 7-8 days for the full unit but does not prescribe a fixed summative duration. The summative should fit flexibly within the unit’s review/assessment time and may be adjusted without changing the Mastery Goals or Learning Ladder.

Meeting Evidence: Meeting does not require perfection. The body of secure evidence should show independent, mathematically correct command of the Mastery Goals with sufficient precision, representation use, and interpretation/justification to support a consistent judgment.

Exceeding Evidence: Exceeding may emerge when a student applies the same unit learning with greater transfer, sophistication, representation synthesis, or justification than the expected challenge; every student does not need a separate “Exceeding question.”

Performance Task Contribution: The optional richer evidence opportunities in this plan may contribute additional Portfolio evidence when used. The conventional secure summative must still assess this plan’s Mastery Goals and Learning Ladder.

Reassessment / Alternate Forms: The framework does not prescribe a reassessment system. If an alternate form is used, preserve the same Mastery Goals, Learning Ladder, representation demands, and recurring-practice expectations while changing surface features and specific prompts.

DOK: DOK is descriptive, not a quota. Do not force DOK 4 or any fixed distribution into the summative; cognitive demand should follow the actual calculus reasoning being assessed.
